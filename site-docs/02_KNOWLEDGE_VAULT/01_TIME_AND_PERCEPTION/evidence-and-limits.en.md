# Evidence and limits: memory and intention

The available project proposal summarizes two research findings relevant to claims about time and perception:

- **Working memory — qualified evidence.** Cowan (2001) argues for a capacity of about four chunks under particular experimental conditions. This is not a universal limit of four individual items or a rule for every task.
- **Readiness potential — qualified evidence.** Libet et al. (1983) compared the reported time of a conscious intention to act with the onset of brain activity in a specific voluntary-movement experiment. The often-cited difference of about 350 ms belongs to that task and its methods. It is not a general delay in perception.

Neither finding establishes the Time Density Paradigm or validates a general theory of subjective time. Treating the Libet result as a universal perception delay would exceed the cited study's scope.
