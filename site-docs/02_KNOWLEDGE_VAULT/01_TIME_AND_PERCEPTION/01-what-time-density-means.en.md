# What does “time density” mean?

“Time density” is a project term for describing how much temporal pressure or activity surrounds a process. It is a way to ask a useful question: **what is happening to a system when many changes, decisions, or demands arrive in a limited time?**

The term does not mean that time is a material, a liquid, or a hidden force. It does not currently have an agreed measuring instrument, unit, or equation. In this repository, it is a working concept that helps us describe situations before we decide whether they can be measured.

## A simple example

Imagine two fifteen-minute walks. During the first, the route is familiar and quiet. During the second, you are late, checking directions, avoiding obstacles, and answering messages. The clock measures the same fifteen minutes. The temporal context is different: the second walk contains more simultaneous demands and more frequent transitions.

Time Density gives us language for that difference. It does not claim that the minutes themselves have changed.

## Four useful building blocks

To discuss the idea clearly, start with four questions:

1. **What is the system?** A person, a team, a device, or a process?
2. **What is its state?** What conditions and options are present right now?
3. **What transitions occur?** What changes from one state to another?
4. **What is the temporal context?** How much activity, urgency, or change surrounds those transitions?

These questions keep the discussion concrete. They also make it possible to disagree about an observation without turning a metaphor into a fact.

## What this concept does not say

Time Density is not, by itself, an explanation of gravity, matter, the speed of light, or cosmology. It is not evidence that perception has one universal delay. Any stronger claim would need precise definitions, a testable method, data, and reliable sources.

For now, treat Time Density as a map for describing temporal situations. A map can help us notice patterns; it does not prove that the territory works according to the map.
